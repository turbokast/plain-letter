## Reading a picture of the letter (on the device)

Recorded 2026-11-16 in headless Chrome 157.0.8103.50 on Linux, NVIDIA RTX 4060 8 GB: each sample letter drawn onto a 1240 x 1754 picture (scripts/photo-eval.js), then read by the on-device model through the Firebase AI Logic SDK (mode only_on_device, image input). One run, not cherry-picked. These are drawn pictures, not phone photos of paper: a real photo will do worse.

| Picture | Letters read | Words read | Labelled details read exactly | Needed facts read exactly | Letters with every label right | Time per letter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean: black on white, straight | 10/10 | 1519/1533 (99.1%) | 64/68 (94.1%) | 42/42 (100.0%) | 7/10 | 14 to 27 s |
| Rough: grey paper, turned 2 degrees, slight blur, shaded | 10/10 | 1524/1533 (99.4%) | 67/68 (98.5%) | 42/42 (100.0%) | 9/10 | 13 to 26 s |

Hiding works on the text that was read, and the picture never leaves the device, so a misread does not expose the true value. But the reply would then carry the misread value. That is why the page asks the person to check the text against the paper.

## Limits

- The patterns were written while looking at these same letters, so the patterns-only figure is optimistic for letters we have not seen.
- Ten letters written by us is a small, friendly sample. Real letters vary more (scans, tables, handwriting, Welsh).
- The labels are ours. A detail we did not think to label is not counted.
- Names without a title and address lines without a street word cannot be found by patterns; that is the on-device model's job, and the user sees the text before it is sent and can hide anything else by hand.
